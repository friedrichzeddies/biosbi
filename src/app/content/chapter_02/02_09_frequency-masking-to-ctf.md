# Masking Frequencies: Your First Hands-On CTF Intuition

Now we stop just observing frequency components and begin intervening. If you suppress specific regions in Fourier space, the real-space image changes in precise and often dramatic ways, and this is one of the cleanest ways to build intuition for what microscope transfer functions do automatically.

When low frequencies are suppressed, broad structure starts to fade and edges dominate; when high frequencies are suppressed, detail softens and blur takes over. This is not just a visual gimmick, it is a direct demonstration that image appearance is tightly coupled to frequency content.

## Try it yourself

In the next interaction, move between low-pass, high-pass, and band-like masks and compare the reconstructed image each time. Try to articulate what kind of information disappears first in each case.

If this feels familiar and a little satisfying, good. You are essentially rehearsing CTF behavior manually before we introduce the full optics model.
